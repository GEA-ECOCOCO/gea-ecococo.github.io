1. Which wastes we use

Two wastes, two solutions

Coconut shell

The problem

It is a bulky agricultural waste that breaks down slowly because of its high lignin content. It piles up or is burned in the open, where combustion is incomplete and produces smoke and particles.

What we do

We carbonize it in a controlled process and turn it into a charcoal briquette.

Result

A local waste becomes a useful fuel, and is not burned in the open.

Used automotive oil

The problem

It contains hydrocarbons, additives and metals released by engine wear. If it is poured on the ground, down a drain or into a river, it forms a film that reduces oxygen in the water and contaminates the soil.

What we do

We reuse it as fuel for our process equipment, instead of letting it be dumped.

Result

A hazardous industrial waste is used as energy.

Wood and firewood

The problem

Traditional charcoal is made from firewood and puts pressure on forest resources.

What we do

We make charcoal from an agricultural waste, not from trees.

Result

An alternative that does not require cutting down forest.

4. Level of pollution and benefit

What pollutes, how much and what we do

Coconut shell

Risk level: moderate and mostly local. It is not a toxic waste, but when piled up it breeds mosquitoes, produces methane as it rots, smoke when burned and blocked waterways.

What GEA ECOCOCO does: we receive it, carbonize it and turn it into a charcoal briquette.

Benefit: less waste piled up or burned in the open, and a fuel made in the region.

Used automotive oil

Risk level: high if disposed of badly. It contains heavy metals and toxic hydrocarbons, and a small amount can contaminate a large volume of water.

What GEA ECOCOCO does: we reuse it as fuel for our process equipment, filtered and with controlled combustion.

Benefit: that oil is no longer dumped and is used as energy, without buying new fuel.

Firewood for charcoal

Risk level: pressure on forest resources when charcoal is made from trees.

What GEA ECOCOCO does: we make charcoal from an agricultural waste.

Benefit: a charcoal alternative that does not require cutting down trees.

What we don't measure yet

We do not yet have our own figures for tonnes of shell used, liters of oil reused or plant emissions. We will publish them in the Plant log once they are measured. We prefer to say so rather than make up numbers.

5. What our process generates

Nothing hidden: all combustion produces emissions

Burning any fuel, including recycled oil, produces carbon dioxide (CO₂), water vapor and, if combustion is incomplete, smoke, particles and carbon monoxide. Carbonizing the shell also releases gases. There is no industrial process without emissions, and we will not claim otherwise.

What we do is reduce and control them from the design stage.

How we control them

  • Controlled combustion: we regulate the air-fuel ratio in the burner, because complete combustion produces less smoke and fewer particles.
  • Oil filtering: used oil is filtered before burning to remove impurities.
  • Carbonization in a closed chamber: the shell is transformed with little oxygen, instead of being burned in the open.
  • Separate equipment and flame safety: designed with controls to prevent combustion failures.
6. Our commitment

What we commit to doing

Circular economy

Every waste in the process is reused as an input or as energy for another step.

Transparency
