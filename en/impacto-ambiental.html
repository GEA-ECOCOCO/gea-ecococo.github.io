Waste that becomes resources

1. Which wastes we use

Priority: coconut shell

Coconut shell

The problem

It is a bulky agricultural by-product that breaks down slowly because of its high lignin content. It piles up or is burned in the open, where combustion is incomplete and produces smoke and particles.

What we do

We carbonize it in a controlled process and turn it into a charcoal briquette.

Result

Objective: for a local waste to become a useful fuel and stop being burned in the open.

Used automotive oil

The problem

It contains hydrocarbons, additives and metals released by engine wear. If it is poured on the ground, down a drain or into a river, it forms a film that reduces oxygen in the water and contaminates the soil.

What we do

We are evaluating its use as an auxiliary fuel for the process, subject to applicable regulations and emissions control.

Result

Under evaluation. We do not present it as a demonstrated environmental benefit until we have measurements and permits.

Wood and firewood

The problem

Traditional charcoal is made from firewood and puts pressure on forest resources.

What we do

We make charcoal from an agricultural by-product, not from trees.

Result

Objective: a charcoal alternative that does not require cutting down forest.

4. Level of pollution and benefit

What pollutes, how much and what we do

Coconut shell

Risk level: moderate and mostly local. It is not a toxic waste, but when piled up it breeds mosquitoes, produces methane as it rots, smoke when burned and blocked waterways.

What GEA ECOCOCO does: we receive it, carbonize it and turn it into a charcoal briquette.

Objective: less waste piled up or burned in the open, and a fuel made in the region. No own measurements yet.

Used automotive oil

Risk level: high if improperly disposed of. Contains heavy metals and toxic hydrocarbons.

GEA status: under evaluation as an auxiliary fuel for the process.

Benefit: not demonstrated. We do not present it as an environmental benefit until we have measurements and permits.

Firewood for charcoal

Risk level: pressure on forest resources when charcoal is made from trees.

What GEA ECOCOCO does: we make charcoal from an agricultural by-product.

Objective: a charcoal alternative that does not require cutting down forest.

What we don't measure yet

We do not yet have our own figures for tons of shell used, consumption or plant emissions. We will publish them in the Logbook once measured. We prefer to say so rather than invent numbers.

5. What our process generates

Nothing hidden: all combustion produces emissions

Burning any fuel produces carbon dioxide (CO₂), water vapor and, if combustion is incomplete, smoke, particles and carbon monoxide. Coconut shell carbonization also releases gases. No industrial process is free of emissions, and we will not say otherwise.

What we do is reduce and control them from the design stage.

How we control them

  • Design objective, controlled combustion: regulate the air-fuel ratio in the burner, because complete combustion produces less smoke and fewer particles.
  • Compliance: we will consult and comply with what MARN and the Ministry of Energy and Mines require before operating.
  • Design objective, closed-chamber carbonization: the shell would be transformed with little oxygen, instead of being burned in the open.
  • Design objective, flame safety: equipment with controls to prevent combustion failures.
6. Our commitment

What we commit to doing

Circular economy

Objective: for the process waste to be used as an input for another step.
