Market

Why there is demand

Public data on the Guatemalan context and on coconut shell charcoal. These are general figures, not GEA results.

3 out of 4 homes

According to ECLAC (CEPAL), three out of four homes in Guatemala cook with firewood. The La Hora article that cites the study says that in rural areas it is nine out of ten. It is an everyday fuel market with room for cleaner alternatives.

Smoke and health

Firewood smoke is linked to respiratory disease, especially in children. That is why businesses that cook all day look for fuels that burn cleaner than firewood.

Coconut charcoal

It is denser and holds heat well; it is reported to reach about 720 °C versus about 700 °C for wood charcoal, with very little flavor of its own. It suits grilling and delicate foods.

Sources: ECLAC (CEPAL), homes cooking with firewood in Guatemala; Tasting Table, wood charcoal vs. coconut charcoal (popular media). The articles do not state the year of the figure; use it as an order of magnitude.

For your business

A product with a story you can tell

If you have a shop, a restaurant or a grill, you offer a charcoal made from local coconut waste, without using firewood as raw material. It is one more reason for your customers to prefer it.

  • Local origin: coconut shell from the South Coast, a waste that would otherwise pile up or be burned.
  • No firewood: a charcoal alternative that does not require cutting down trees.
  • Ignition time: approximate, 5 to 10 minutes, based on tests carried out by GEA.
  • For your customers: you can tell them where what they buy comes from.
